## 🧠 What's the big idea?

Gears are invisible magic to a 7-year-old — turn one, and somehow another one
far away spins too, faster or slower, stronger or weaker. **Cheese Heist makes
that magic visible.**

The child builds a real LEGO Technic crane. The iPad *sees* the actual gears
through the camera, drops a glowing virtual twin right on top of them, and
lets the child crank it live — no formulas, no numbers, no "ratio" ever
spoken out loud. Just cheese going up **fast & weak**, or **slow & strong**,
depending on what they built. 🐭⚙️🧀

- 🔍 **Real gears, real detection** — Core ML spots the child's own LEGO gears through the camera
- ✨ **A virtual twin, locked in AR** — spins in perfect sync with the physical build
- 🎮 **One crank, instant feedback** — turn it and *feel* the speed/strength trade-off
- 🐈 **A cat on the prowl** — because a heist needs stakes
- 📖 **Zero reading required** — every idea is taught by doing, never by text

speed, rope kinematics, run in SI units every single frame — the child just
never sees a number. LiDAR is non-negotiable: it's what lets the app find a
plain classroom table instantly, place the virtual gear exactly on the
physical one, and let the mouse duck convincingly behind a real LEGO brick.
